# Richard J. Allen Professional Portfolio — Visual Engagement Refresh Plan

## Objective

Keep the current application-routing architecture and factual/source-control discipline, while making the site noticeably more interesting, memorable, and pleasant for human visitors to explore.

The redesign should help a recruiter or hiring manager answer these questions quickly:

1. What does Richard do?
2. What has he actually led?
3. What scale has he handled?
4. What are the strongest examples of his work?
5. Where should I click next for the role I am hiring for?
6. How do I contact him?

The site should feel like a professional leadership portfolio, not a résumé converted into cards.

## Research takeaways

Contemporary successful portfolio sites consistently use a few patterns worth borrowing:

- Put selected work on the homepage instead of requiring visitors to hunt for it.
- Lead with a concise positioning statement, then show proof immediately.
- Curate a small number of strong projects/case studies rather than presenting everything equally.
- Make project previews visual: image, diagram, before/after state, process flow, or strong outcome metric.
- Give every case study a clear problem, Richard's role, action/process, and result.
- Use a professional portrait or strong personal-brand visual when available.
- Use color, contrast, typography, spacing, and section variation to create rhythm and hierarchy.
- Keep navigation simple and contact actions easy to find.
- Use subtle interaction/hover behavior to reward exploration without becoming gimmicky.
- Keep the experience fast, responsive, and readable on mobile.

## Current visual weaknesses

- Too many white cards with similar visual weight.
- Most sections are text-first and visually interchangeable.
- Strong accomplishments are present but do not create enough visual drama.
- Case studies are mostly prose; there are few memorable visual anchors.
- Homepage does not preview the strongest work deeply enough.
- Limited personal presence / human identity beyond Richard's name and copy.
- Repeated grid structures make long pages feel predictable.
- Important career moments compete equally with supporting details.

## Proposed visual direction

### 1. Hero: leadership portfolio, not résumé header

Retain the current positioning, but shift to a split editorial hero.

Left:
- Richard J. Allen
- short outcome-driven statement
- 1–2 sentence positioning copy
- primary CTAs: Selected Work / Resume / Contact

Right:
- preferred option: professional portrait if Richard supplies/approves one
- fallback: a branded operational-system graphic built from Richard's actual themes:
  People → Workflow → Measures → Improvement

Use a stronger navy/teal background treatment or soft gradient rather than a mostly-white opening.

### 2. Replace four equal stat cards with a visual proof strip

Primary proof:
- 32 support & technical-support representatives managed
- 125+ SOP program
- 400+ workforce position records
- 20+ recurring/ad-hoc workforce reports

Use large numerals, short labels, thin dividers, and alternating accent colors. Avoid four identical white boxes.

### 3. Put Featured Work directly on the homepage

Show three flagship case-study previews with distinct visual treatments:

#### A. People Operations at Scale — HawkSoft
Visual idea: simplified team/org graphic or management-system diagram.
Proof: 32 representatives managed after promotion to Customer Support Manager.

#### B. SOP Modernization & Governance — OED
Visual idea: before → after diagram:
Scattered procedures → standards/review → centralized governed library.
Proof: year-long program; 125+ SOPs; 25+ legacy procedures inventoried and updated.

#### C. Hosted-Service Migration — HawkSoft
Visual idea: migration flow / agency nodes moving from legacy environment to replacement provider.
Proof: hundreds of agencies; completed at least one month ahead of critical deadline.

Each preview should contain:
- challenge
- Richard's role
- one strong result
- View case study CTA

### 4. Add a compact Career Arc

A visual timeline should show the management/operations progression without reproducing the full résumé:

Aramark Warehouse Manager → HawkSoft Team Lead → Customer Support Manager → Customer Experience Manager → OED/OHA Operations & Policy work → Marion County operations.

The goal is to make the leadership arc visible in seconds.

### 5. Give each role-specific page a unique visual anchor

Customer Success:
- Customer Journey lifecycle diagram
- 32-person leadership proof
- feedback / enablement loop

Operations & Leadership:
- People → Workflow → Measures → Improve operating model
- manager scope and reporting scale

Process Improvement:
- SOP governance lifecycle / before-after model
- repository + standards + review controls

Implementation & Enablement:
- migration / change-adoption journey
- hundreds of agencies / one-month-early / six-month-readiness proof ribbon

The page should not rely on repeated card grids alone.

### 6. Upgrade Case Studies from cards to visual stories

Keep the existing case-study structure but make the top 3–4 flagship stories richer.

For each flagship case study:
- strong visual header
- 1-line challenge
- Richard's role/ownership
- visual process or timeline
- measurable result / outcome
- key skills/tags

Supporting case studies can remain compact.

### 7. Use sanitized evidence visuals

Continue the approach already used on Community Leadership:
- do not expose raw internal/customer/private records
- reconstruct diagrams, dashboards, process maps, planning documents, or reporting artifacts using only non-sensitive information
- clearly label reconstructions when appropriate

Potential work-sample visuals:
- SOP governance flow
- management reporting/dashboard concept
- Customer Journey map
- migration timeline
- recurring operations cadence
- functional-testing / release-readiness checklist

### 8. Stronger section rhythm

Alternate page treatments to reduce visual monotony:
- white editorial section
- soft tinted section
- navy/teal feature band
- large-number proof strip
- visual case-study section
- quote/callout section

Avoid stacking six visually identical card grids.

### 9. Personality without gimmicks

Allowed:
- stronger accent colors
- asymmetric layout
- subtle gradients
- simple line icons
- CSS diagrams
- hover elevation / border movement
- restrained scroll/entrance transitions if performance remains excellent
- professional portrait

Avoid:
- stock-photo filler
- animated backgrounds that distract from content
- excessive parallax
- novelty cursors
- autoplay video/audio
- decorative animation with no information value
- visual effects that reduce recruiter scanability

### 10. Mobile requirements

- Preserve the horizontal-scroll primary navigation currently implemented.
- Featured case-study cards must stack cleanly.
- Proof strip should collapse to two columns, then one column if needed.
- Diagrams must remain readable without horizontal page scrolling.
- Key CTA should remain visible near the top.
- Avoid small infographic text.

## Visual language

Keep the current professional palette but use it more assertively:

- Deep navy: primary leadership/authority color
- Teal: people/customer/process improvement
- Warm gold: measurable impact / outcomes
- Violet: change, enablement, cross-functional work
- Off-white / soft gray: editorial breathing room

Typography:
- Keep a highly readable sans-serif body font.
- Consider a stronger display face or heavier editorial treatment for major headings, while remaining fast and accessible.

Icons:
- Use a small coherent set of simple line icons or CSS-drawn symbols.
- Icons should clarify category/function, not decorate every bullet.

## Source-control guardrail

Every number, scope statement, title, date, result, testimonial, artifact, diagram, or case-study claim must remain traceable to Richard's Career Master Facts & Accomplishment Bank, Historical Master Resume & Career Record, confirmed interview/work-product evidence, or Richard's direct confirmation.

Visual storytelling may simplify presentation, but must never exaggerate the underlying fact.
